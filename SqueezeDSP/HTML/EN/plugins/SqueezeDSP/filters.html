SqueezeDSP Filter Guide

Each filter shapes the treble and sharp sounds (drum hits, plucked strings) its own way. Choose by ear and by the music. The figures describe each one; they do not rank them.

DSD decoding

DSD carries strong noise above the music; the low-pass removes it. With DSD out, the DSP rate sets the route. With PCM out, a DSD file is turned into PCM.

DSP rateWhat happens to a DSD file
DXD 352.8 kHz
176.4 kHz		Our decoder turns it into PCM at that rate. The DSP works there, then the modulator makes DSD again.
NativeIt stays at its own DSD rate (2.8 MHz for DSD64) and never goes down to a PCM rate. The integrator removes the noise, the DSP works at the DSD rate, then the modulator makes DSD again. DSF files only: DFF and WavPack DSD files, and a DSD rate out below the file's own, take the DXD route.

Native needs a fast computer. DSD Native DSP needs a fast multi-core desktop Intel processor or an Apple Silicon (M-series) Mac. Speed varies with each setup (see Speed across rates). Tested: a Mac mini M6 runs Native at DSD256 in and out at 2× real time.

Modulators

The modulator turns the DSP's output back into DSD. Every modulator adds a little noise; it pushes most of it above the music. Each has two tunings, picked for you: one for PCM sources, one for DSD sources (DSD recordings can be louder).

  • Figures: noise each modulator adds in the audio band (20 Hz–20 kHz), in dB, with a 1 kHz test tone. Lower is quieter. For scale: a CD's own noise floor is about −98 dB, a 24-bit recording's about −146 dB.
  • On 10 real DSD64 recordings, decoded and made into DSD64 again: Standard −120, CLANS −127 to −128, Adaptive −135 to −140.
  • The quiet zone above the music widens with each step up in DSD rate.
  • CPU: the modulator's own work, relative to Standard. The whole route costs more at higher DSD rates: DSD256 out took 2.7× the CPU of DSD64 out on the same track.

How we tested

  • Every SRC filter measured at 44.1 kHz and 96 kHz, the first family cross-checked in MATLAB. Kaiser and Gaussian remove images by 170 dB or more.
  • Rate pairs tested: up, down and odd ratios (such as 44.1 to 48 kHz), up to 768 kHz.
  • Gapless: tracks join byte for byte with every filter family tested.
  • Modulators: 7 test signals × 4 DSD rates × 3 modulators at full level, 10 real DSD recordings (DSD64 to DSD512), and live playback on the test server. 0 resets.
  • Live by ear on several players, including an Eversolo DMP-A8 and a FiiO M11 Plus.
